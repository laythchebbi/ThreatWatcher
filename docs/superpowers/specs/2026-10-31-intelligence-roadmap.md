# Intelligence roadmap — 2026-10-31

Three research agents looked at (1) external threat-intel feeds and
infrastructure enrichment, (2) attacker-infrastructure correlation, phishing-kit
fingerprinting and LLM use, (3) surfaces outside lookalike domains. This
document ranks what Threat Watcher should add on top of the v2 design
(`2026-10-30-threat-watcher-v2-design.md`) and its Plans 1–3. Claims cite the
page they came from; items the agents could not read are marked _(unverified)_.
Nothing here is built; "Plan 4" and "Plan 5" are proposals.

## 1. The shape of the next two plans

| Plan                           | Theme                                                                    | Why this order                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plan 4 — Page intelligence** | Make the "live phishing page" alert trustworthy and name the kit         | It upgrades the signal the product already produces; every item is a function over the HTTP fetch Plan 2 adds. |
| **Plan 5 — New surfaces**      | DMARC reports, report-a-phish inbox, campaign clustering, exposure panel | New inputs, new events; each depends on inbound mail or on several cycles of stored data.                      |

Costs for item 9 at 2026-10-31 prices (Haiku 5.5 $0.10 in / $0.50 out per
MTok; Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10): summary 800 in + 120 out → $0.14 per 1 000 events
on Haiku; takedown draft 1 500 in + 400 out → $7 per 1 000 on Sonnet, so about
a cent per incident.

**LLM page classification is deferred.** Hidden-text prompt injection flips a
frontier model's phishing verdict 40 % to 85 % of the time
([arXiv 2602.05484](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05484)). If it is ever added:
strip scripts, hidden elements and comments, cap at 4 k tokens, wrap as
untrusted data, structured enum output validated with zod, no tools, one
weighted feature among deterministic ones, never allowed to lower severity.
Autonomous LLM triage with tool access is out at every stage.

## 4. Do not build, and why

- **LinkedIn, Instagram/Facebook, TikTok, Telegram channel discovery, APKPure/APKMirror:** no API or contractually banned scraping; LinkedIn won its hiQ case and the injunction stands ([LinkedIn terms](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)).
- **X monitoring:** pay-per-use only and the surveillance clause in the restricted-use cases; revisit only on customer demand ([X pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)).
- **AlienVault OTX, abuse.ch ThreatFox/URLhaus free tier, Spamhaus free DQS, SURBL/URIBL, ip-api.com, Shodan InternetDB:** non-commercial terms ([OTX EULA](https://www.levelblue.com/terms/otx-eula), [abuse.ch terms](https://abuse.ch/terms-of-use/), [SURBL](https://www.surbl.org/usage-policy), [Shodan](https://blog.shodan.io/introducing-the-internetdb-api/)). abuse.ch's SSLBL alone is CC0 and fine.
- **RIPEstat** needs written permission for paid products; it is one email, so ask before wiring it in ([terms](https://www.ripe.net/about-us/legal/ripestat-service-terms-and-conditions/)).
- **JA4+ fingerprints** carry the FoxIO licence that blocks selling them inside a product; our own SPKI hashing covers the useful part ([FAQ](https://github.com/FoxIO-LLC/ja4/blob/main/License%20FAQ.md)). JARM is weak alone (fingerprints the TLS stack, not the operator).
- **Google Custom Search** is closed to new customers and ends 2027-01-01; **Bing Web Search** retired in 2025; **BGPView** no longer resolves.
- **Deepfake or whaling "monitoring", executive display-name spoof detection:** no public pre-attack signal; needs mailbox access. The report-a-phish inbox with a VIP name list is the honest version.
- **Autonomous LLM triage** with tool access over attacker content.

## 5. Changes this implies for the v2 design

1. Plan 2's HTTP step stores the leaf certificate's `fingerprint256` and SPKI hash, the redirect chain hosts, and the response headers, since Plan 4 items 2, 3 and 8 read them.
2. The `Sighting.http` placeholder becomes `{ status, title, headers, redirects[], hasPasswordForm, formSignals[], brandTermFound, cloakingDiff, kit, similarity }`.
3. `events.kind` gains `page_mimics_login`'s siblings: `kit_identified`, `confirmed_phishing`, `campaign_linked`, `dmarc_spoof`, `dmarc_lookalike_sender`, `reported_lure`, `posture_weak`.
4. Alert content (spec 7.5) adds hosting platform + abuse desk, ASN, and the kit name when known.
5. `domains.terms[]` doubles as the brand-term list for app-store and SERP checks.

Not checked: no endpoint was exercised from this repo; prices and tiers are as of 2026-10-31. Risk: kit indicator strings rot within months, so every list in Plan 4 is data refreshed nightly, never code.
